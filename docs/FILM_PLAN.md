# Launch film

I am making a TwitchCon product launch film with a 2:45 target runtime. The film uses the
working project, real Twitch evidence and the recorded Codex build. Its pace comes from
the action: claim, hold, receipts, reveal, replay.

## Direction

I use bold type, dark stage space, sharp crops, animated chat lines and cuts on the beat.
Twitch community language drives the transitions: “Chat, is this real?”, “Show the receipts”,
“Chat called it” and “Check the VOD”. Purple and white can frame the Twitch context;
the product keeps its cyan replay-booth identity and evidence colours.

I write the scripted chat for the film and gallery in the voice of chat: shorthand, typos,
emote codes and quick reactions. These examples can differ from the evaluation set. I record
their results through the actual pipeline before capture. I include one gallery image with
a French claim and a Spanish claim, their verified excerpts and final verdicts. I keep
their counts separate from evaluation metrics.

I build original motion graphics and sound design. Source captions and product screens
remain readable between the fast transitions. The film presents an independent project
built for Twitch, with the project name on the opening and closing cards.

| 2:12–2:33 | Built with Codex | Actual build footage, the agent instructions, a concrete bug and its fix, and passing checks. Keep the build evidence visible. |
| 2:33–2:45 | The call | Return to the three product actions. End on the project name and the working public demo address. |

## Production

- Build an editable timeline and reusable motion scenes before the final render.
- Capture the finished interface and Twitch flow after the UI walkthrough passes.
- Use original audio or audio with a recorded licence. Mix the narration clearly above the bed.
- Export a 1080p master, an upload-ready MP4, English captions and a clean thumbnail.
- Review the full film at normal speed for pacing, text legibility, audio and factual claims.
- Keep recordings, rendered frames and video files outside version control.

The [contest rules](https://twitchcon-codex.devpost.com/rules) require a video under three
minutes with footage of the Twitch integration and the Codex build. They also require
permission for third-party marks and copyrighted material. The [official brand source](https://brand.twitch.com/)
is the reference for any permitted Twitch assets. Asset permissions are recorded with the edit.
